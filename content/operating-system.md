Operating System · Practice principles

# I don't want a team that executes.
I want a team of owners.

Recognized, proactive - experts with a name, a face, not just a pair of hands. It runs on one conviction: **a system carries what repeats, so people carry what matters**. Design tokens keep experiences consistent across touchpoints; distributed ownership makes people autonomous.

**One variable in common: trust. One prerequisite: top to frontline, everyone gets to own their part.**

Nothing ships without discovery.

Nothing ships on a hunch. Every component, every line of roadmap starts with the same work: what does the product ecosystem actually need, and what already exists to build on? **Need first, existing second, decision third.**

It's the discipline I put in place after learning, the hard way, what it costs to build for the brand instead of the people who adopt it. **Discovery isn't a phase. It's the gate.**

At Sanofi, it's how we knew what to build versus what to buy - and it didn't stop at launch: we kept interviewing, testing our own ways of working, **reprioritizing against what we heard**.

One source of truth - trusted by humans, verified by machines.

One language, not four. **Multi-surface design tokens** carry brand and behavior across web, native and content; **Figma variables** hold the component architecture on the design side, **Storybook** holds it on the code side.

And trust doesn't mean unchecked: **automate the repeatable, measure what matters, keep the human judgment where it counts**. That's governance you can't skip and don't have to police.

At Sanofi, every pull request ran **automated conformity checks before a double human validation**, Chromatic caught visual regressions before they shipped, and I read DORA metrics every sprint next to satisfaction surveys - as decisions, not dashboards for show.

Accessibility is the floor. Responsibility is the reach.

**WCAG AA is a floor, not a bar to clear**: the minimum for every product, not just the design system. Accessibility protects the user. Responsibility protects everything around them. **Same driver - inclusion - different reach.**

For example at Sanofi, I positioned it as the digital standard, we audited the full library to **100% compliance before our first major**, then held it there through continuous review - axe embedded, Storybook's native checks, manual review on top - **so no product team paid the accessibility cost downstream**.

Inclusion doesn't stop at who can *use* the product. A system touching dozens of products has a footprint - societal and environmental - and it isn't small. **Responsibility gets designed in, not bolted on**, and it looks like sobriety at every level.

- Light by default. Modular imports, minimal weight - teams pulled what they needed, not the whole library. **Lighter reaches further.**

- Quiet by design. Sober by choice, so brands express themselves without fighting it. **Restraint as a feature, not a limitation.**

- Flexible by architecture. The smallest set that meets the widest need. **Fewer components, more coverage.**

AI should earn trust, not exploit it.

When AI enters a roadmap, most teams ask what it can *do*. I ask what it should *never* do, and I put that on the table first: investigate, test before rolling anything at scale, and **treat it as a tool added to the stack with responsible usage** - not a technology to embrace because it exists.

An AI that nudges, manipulates, anchors on dark patterns or manufactures urgency isn't a feature, it's a breach of trust - and in healthcare that trust isn't abstract. It starts with an employee and it finishes with a patient.

Not everyone shares this as a priority. I do. Coherent and on-brand is the baseline. **Ethical is the standard.**

We break nothing without a bridge.

A careless breaking change is a tax paid once per consumer, so the cadence is deliberate: **one major release a year, maximum**, with a steady run of improvements and fixes in between. And when one is unavoidable, it ships with a **patch path** and a real buffer for teams to upgrade on their own schedule.

At Sanofi, forty-five products depended on the library - that tax, paid forty-five times. **A bridge, every time.**

Support on their terms, not mine.

The system serves the people building on it, so **support flexes to them, not the other way around**: office hours for whoever wants synchronous help, asynchronous support for whoever needs their own clock - teams keep control over the urgency of their own problems.

And communication is built to outgrow me: **the community takes the mic**, not me.

At Sanofi, members carried the messages and answered each other directly, becoming the channel instead of routing everything through me.

A team of owners, not a team of hands.

**Ownership is structural, not a pep talk.** Roles rotate, planning is collective, retros run every sprint - so recognition moves around and **no one gets stuck in execution**.

At Sanofi, the support lead changed every sprint - a model we iterated into by reading the support data until the rotation was what the signal asked for. All of it documented, so a new joiner became an owner fast: **transparency isn't a value on a wall, it's the onboarding**.

And growth runs through the community, not top-down - **reverse mentoring by practice**, where I grow as much through them as they do through me. *I lead, and I stay a student.*

## That's not the soft version of leadership. *It's systemic operations with care.*

Seven principles, each with a practice behind it - and each one designed to keep working the day I stop running it. That's the how. The mindset that picks them is the other half: what I hold about craft, trust, and the people a system is for.
